About Geary Park

The borough history page says suburban development took off after World War II, notably with Geary Park and new housing across the north side. South Plainfield grew from 5,047 residents in 1930 to 17,879 in 1960, with its biggest jump in the 1950s.

A real estate listing site describes Geary Park as single-family homes built between 1951 and 1960. That is supporting evidence only. We do not have official boundaries, so we describe the area by its anchor street, Geary Drive.

A house from the 1950s: what to ask

A house of that age has usually seen several owners and several renovations. The original plumbing may have been replaced, partly replaced or left alone, and you cannot tell from the street. Do not assume a material or a problem from the year alone.

A good first step is a visit that checks the shutoff valves, the heater, the visible supply lines and the main drain. A camera look at the sewer line answers the question that age raises, and sewer camera inspection shows the pipe itself.

Water heater near its end of life

Tank heaters have a limited life, and a leak can ruin a finished basement. See water heater replacement and water heater installation.

Slow kitchen or bath drain

Grease, hair and soap build up in any house. Drain cleaning clears the line and shows whether it comes back.

Dripping faucet or running toilet

Small leaks waste water and raise the bill. See faucet repair and toilet repair.

Low pressure at several fixtures

Pressure that drops everywhere can point to scaled lines or a failing valve. Leak detection rules out a hidden leak.

Hard water in South Plainfield

Middlesex Water serves portions of the borough, and its 2025 report shows hardness averaging 157 ppm, ranging from 90 to 308 ppm. Hard water leaves scale on heater elements, faucet screens and showerheads.

Check your bill to see who supplies your address. A heater flush once a year is a simple way to slow scale, and a plumber can tell you whether a softener makes sense.
