Tankless Water Heater Service in South Plainfield, NJ

Tankless heaters promise endless hot water, but they ask more of the house than a tank does. Gas or electrical capacity, venting, water quality and a regular flush all matter, and hard water is the one that catches people out.

Installing a tankless water heater

A tankless heater needs a gas line or an electrical circuit that can feed it, a vent that meets the manufacturer's rules, and supply lines sized to carry the flow. Many South Plainfield homes were built for tank heaters, so the existing gas pipe or panel is the first thing to check. If the supply is too small, the unit will shut off or deliver lukewarm water when two showers run.

A plumber measures what the house has, tells you what changes are needed and gives you a clear price before work begins. An electric tankless unit trades gas piping for heavy electrical load, which sometimes means a panel upgrade.

Flushing and descaling in hard water

The heat exchanger in a tankless unit is a narrow passage, and hard water coats it with scale over time. Middlesex Water, which serves portions of South Plainfield, reports average hardness of 157 ppm with a range from 90 to 308 ppm in its 2025 report. That is roughly 9 grains per gallon on average, enough to justify a flush on a schedule rather than waiting for an error code.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit using a small pump and isolation valves. If those isolation valves were never installed, the plumber can add them, which makes every future flush quick. Ask for the manufacturer's recommended interval for your model and water.

Error codes appear

Many codes point to scale, a blocked vent, low flow or a gas supply problem.

Hot water goes cold in the middle of a shower

This can mean the unit is undersized, scaled or shutting down on a safety limit.

Water takes longer to get hot

A recirculation pump or a small buffer tank can help in a long run.

Flow seems weaker

A clogged inlet screen or scaled heat exchanger restricts flow.

Repairs and recirculation

Common tankless repairs include cleaning inlet screens, replacing flow sensors or igniters, and clearing a blocked condensate drain on high-efficiency models. Electric units can also have a failed heating element, which is a part you can see listed under the manufacturer's service guide. If the heater feeds a boiler loop or the unit is in a basement that floods, mention it when you call.

A recirculation pump brings hot water to the far fixtures faster, which cuts the wait and the water wasted. It needs a return line or a crossover valve, and the plumber confirms which your house can use.

Code and standards note

Tankless units are governed by the manufacturer's installation guidelines for venting, clearances and gas or electrical capacity, in addition to the state plumbing code. A plumber should work from the model's own installation manual, not from habit.

What to do if the unit shuts down or leaks

  • Turn off the breaker or the gas supply to the unit and close the water valves.
  • Do not open the cabinet or touch the wiring.
  • Note the error code on the display and the model number.
  • Keep the area dry and stay clear of any water near the electrical supply.
  • Call and share the code and what you saw before the unit stopped.

A tankless problem often starts as a failing tank, so see water heater replacement if you are deciding between the two, or repair if the existing unit can be saved. We service tankless units in Edison and across the area.
