Sewer Camera Inspection in South Plainfield, NJ

A sewer camera turns a guess into a picture. Instead of cabling a line over and over, a plumber feeds a small camera through the pipe and sees the crack, the root mass or the sagging section for themselves.

We use camera inspections before repairs, before buying a home and any time a drain keeps coming back.

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A waterproof camera on a flexible rod goes in through a clean-out or a pulled toilet and travels the length of the line. The plumber watches the image live and notes anything unusual: cracks, offset joints, root intrusion, grease, sagging sections that hold water and the point where a lateral meets the main.

A locator in the camera head sends a signal that lets the plumber mark the exact spot and depth above ground. That matters when a repair is needed, because a small excavation costs far less than a trench along the whole yard.

When to book one

Repeat clogs

A line that clogs several times a year usually has a cause the cable does not reach.

Buying a house

A camera look before closing reveals a failing lateral that a general home inspection does not.

Slow drains throughout

A main line problem shows up in every fixture, not just one.

Wet or sunken yard

Water from a broken line can soften soil over the pipe.

Camera work in older homes

In older houses the pipe material tells a story. Cast iron often shows heavy scale and rough walls, clay shows root entry at joints, and a plastic line from a later renovation may be fine while the old section it connects to is not. A plumber will tell you what each finding means and whether it is cosmetic or urgent.

After a basement flood, a camera look helps decide whether the water came from the street system or from a failure on your side. The result matters for deciding between jetting and a repair, and it gives you something to show a contractor or an insurer.

Code and standards note

Plumbing code standards call for drain pipe to hold a minimum slope, so a camera that shows standing water in a run tells you the pipe has settled. Recording the footage gives you an objective record for any repair decision.

Inspections are available in Plainfield as well, and what the camera finds decides whether the next step is cleaning or repair.

Reading the camera report

A good inspection ends with a clear summary. The plumber should be able to tell you where each finding is, how far from the clean-out it sits and how deep the pipe runs. A short video clip of each problem and a sketch of the line make the next steps easier to price and compare.

If the camera finds only light scale or a small amount of roots, a cleaning schedule may be enough. If it finds a break or a belly, the repair path is covered on our sewer line repair page, and you can use the recording to get a second quote.

Buying or selling a home in South Plainfield

Buyers often schedule a sewer camera look during the inspection period, since a failed lateral can cost more than many repairs combined. Sellers sometimes get one first to avoid surprises. The recording shows the pipe material and condition, and it helps both sides agree on a fair repair credit.

Ask the borough whether any local rule applies to the sewer connection at the time of sale. A plumber can tell you what to look for and how to read the findings.
