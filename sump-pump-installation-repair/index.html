When a sump pump fails in a storm

If the pit is full and the pump is silent or humming without moving water, act quickly. Check that the pump is plugged in and the outlet breaker has not tripped, then lift the float to see if the pump starts. If water is rising near outlets or the panel, stay out of the basement and call the 24/7 line.

A plumber can bring a replacement pump and replace it in one visit. If the problem is a stuck float, a clogged intake or a bad check valve, those parts are quick fixes.

Why South Plainfield basements need working pumps

Hurricane Ida in September 2021 flooded homes across South Plainfield, including a family home that took up to nine feet of water according to News12. Not every basement sees that, but many sit below the water table in wet seasons, and a pump is the line between a damp floor and a flooded one.

Outages come with storms, so a pump that depends only on household power may stop when it matters most. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps the pit pumped while the grid is down.

Discharge and drainage details

A pump needs a discharge line that moves water well away from the foundation. Water sent next to the house just returns to the pit. Check valves keep water from flowing back, and freezing in an exposed discharge line can block a pump in winter.

Rules on where a sump can discharge vary by municipality, so ask the South Plainfield construction office before changing it. A sump that sends water into the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed.

Code and standards note

Sump discharge rules are set locally, and the standard advice is to send water away from the foundation and never into the sanitary sewer. Ask the South Plainfield construction office what applies to your lot.
