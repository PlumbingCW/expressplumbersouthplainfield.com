Signs the main sewer line is blocked

One slow sink is a branch problem. When the tub gurgles as the washer drains, the toilet bubbles when you run the shower or a basement floor drain backs up, the blockage is in the main. Stop running water immediately, since each gallon adds to what is trapped.

Call the 24/7 line from a dry spot. The dispatcher will ask which drains are affected and what the basement looks like, which helps decide how to bring the machine in.

What usually causes a main line clog

Three causes account for most main line clogs: roots entering at a joint, grease and soap that has hardened on the pipe wall, and a broken or bellied section that catches solids. Wipes labeled flushable, paper towels and feminine products add to the problem in every home.

Roots are the one that returns. A cable cuts them back, but they grow again unless the joint is repaired or the pipe is lined. If a line needs cleaning more than once a year, a camera look and a repair are usually cheaper than repeated visits.

Local conditions behind backups

Heavy rain puts load on any municipal sewer, and homes with low basement drains tend to feel a surcharge first. After Hurricane Ida in September 2021 flooded homes in South Plainfield, many owners learned that lesson. A plumber can tell you whether the blockage is on your side or at the connection to the main.

Where a house is older, the lateral may be clay, and clay joints are where roots get in. A cleaning gives a short reset, but a camera look shows how much life the pipe has left.

Code and standards note

According to standard plumbing practice, a main line should be cleaned through a cleanout, not by pulling a toilet unless there is no choice. A plumber who asks where your cleanout is knows the job.

Main line cleaning also covers Scotch Plains, and after the line is clear a camera inspection shows whether it needs repair to stay that way.
