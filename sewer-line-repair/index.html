Emergency Sewer Line Repair in South Plainfield, NJ

A sewer line that has cracked, collapsed or filled with roots does not clear with a cable for long. Wastewater backs up into the lowest drain in the house, usually in the basement, and it will keep coming back until the pipe itself is fixed.

Our 24/7 line sends a plumber to stop the backup, find the failure with a camera and give you repair options with a clear price.

When a sewer line failure is an emergency

Sewage in a tub, a floor drain or the basement is a health hazard and an emergency. Stop running water, keep people and pets away from the wet area, and call the 24/7 line. Do not use drain chemicals, which can burn a plumber's skin and do nothing for a broken pipe.

A plumber will clear the line to get you working again if that is possible, then run a camera to see why it failed. Ask about after-hours timing when you call.

Repair options after the camera finds the problem

The camera shows whether the line has a single break, a bellied section holding water, root intrusion at a joint or a long run of failing pipe. A short break may be fixed with a spot repair, which means digging only over that section. Longer failures call for trenchless pipe lining or a full replacement.

Trenchless lining slides a resin-soaked liner through the old pipe and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one. It avoids digging up a lawn or driveway, but it needs a line that is still round enough to take the liner. A collapsed section has to be dug out.

Older pipe in older South Plainfield streets

Many South Plainfield homes were built across the twentieth century, and the sewer laterals under them range from clay and cast iron to later plastic. Clay joints let roots in, and cast iron scales and thins. The borough was incorporated in 1926, so some of the pipe under older streets has had a long time to age.

Heavy storms add load to the municipal system. After Hurricane Ida in September 2021 flooded homes across the borough, many basement drains and sewer connections were checked. Ask a plumber about a backwater valve if your basement sits low. Our hydro jetting service is the follow-up if the line is sound but coated.

Code and standards note

New Jersey's plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, published by the International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials. Plumbing codes set a minimum slope for drain pipe, which is why a belly in an old lateral shows up on camera as standing water. Permit rules vary, so ask the borough before digging.

A broken lateral in Edison or South Plainfield starts the same way, with a camera inspection, and a line that is sound but coated may only need cleaning.
