A faucet that drips wastes water and wears out the sink. A faucet that leaks under the sink damages the cabinet. Most faucet problems come down to a worn cartridge, washer or seal.

Why faucets drip and leak

Inside a faucet, a cartridge, washer or ball valve controls the flow. As those parts wear, water slips past and drips. Mineral buildup from hard water speeds this up. Middlesex Water, which serves portions of South Plainfield, reports average hardness of 157 ppm in its 2025 report, which is enough to coat aerators and wear seals over the years.

A drip from the spout is usually a worn cartridge or washer. A leak from the handle points to an O-ring or a loose packing nut. Water at the base comes from a failed seal and needs more care.

Low flow and spray problems

Low flow at a single faucet usually means a clogged aerator. Unscrew it, rinse out the grit and soak it in vinegar. If flow is low across the house, the problem is deeper, and our repiping page covers old galvanized lines that narrow with age.

A pull-out sprayer that will not retract or a diverter that sends water to the wrong place is a mechanical fault that a plumber can repair or replace as a part.

Repair or replace

A newer faucet with a worn cartridge is worth repairing. An older one with corroded parts, a finish that is wearing off or parts that are no longer made is often better replaced. A plumber can tell you which category yours is in.

While the sink is open, it is worth checking the shutoff valves and supply lines. Old shutoffs that stick or weep are a good reason to add new valves during the same visit.

Code and standards note

The EPA WaterSense standard for bathroom faucets is a flow of 1.5 gallons per minute or less. A faucet that has lost pressure may be clogged, but a new labeled model is a way to cut use without losing comfort.

Faucet repairs are booked from Scotch Plains as well, and a faucet that is wearing out alongside its valves is a good time for new shutoffs.

Kitchen, bath, laundry and outdoor faucets

Kitchen faucets work harder than any others. Pull-down sprayers, single-handle cartridges and water filter taps all add parts that wear, and a replacement is often cleaner than a repair when the finish is also failing. Bathroom faucets are usually simpler, though widespread sets with separate handles have more seals that can leak.

Laundry sinks and utility faucets are often the oldest in the house, and hose bibs outside need a different eye. A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm wall, and one that drips inside the wall after winter may have split. Disconnect hoses before the first freeze to protect the valve.

Small signs worth acting on

A faucet that drips only when hot water is on points to a worn hot-side cartridge. A faucet that sprays sideways is clogged with mineral scale. A handle that is stiff or loose often means the packing nut or cartridge needs service. Catching these early prevents water from creeping into the cabinet.

If a repair is not worthwhile, a plumber can match the faucet to your sink holes and give you a clear price for the new fixture and its installation.
