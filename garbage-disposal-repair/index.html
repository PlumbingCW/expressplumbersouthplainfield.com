What to try before calling

1

Turn off the power

Switch off the disposal and the wall switch before you reach near it.

2

Use the hex key

Most disposals have a slot in the bottom for a hex wrench to free a jam.

3

Press the reset

A red reset button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats.

4

Check the drain

If water backs up in the other basin, the clog is in the drain line.

Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off at the switch. Use tongs or pliers to remove anything stuck.

Common disposal faults

A humming disposal that does not spin is jammed. A disposal that does nothing is either tripped on its overload or has lost power. A leak from the bottom means the seals have failed and the unit has to be replaced. A leak from the top or sides is often a loose mounting flange or drain connection, which can be reseated.

Slow drains behind a disposal are usually grease and food scraps stuck in the trap or branch line, and the unit itself is fine. Grease poured down the sink is the biggest cause. Our drain cleaning service clears these.

Turn off the power and call. A plumber can tell you whether it can be repaired.

Repair or replace

A disposal that is five to ten years old and leaking is usually not worth repairing. A jammed one is. A plumber can also install a bigger unit for a household that uses the disposal heavily, or a model with a quieter motor.

Many South Plainfield kitchens share a drain with a dishwasher, and a clog in the shared line can show up as dishwasher water in the sink. Fixing the drain is the answer, not the disposal.

Code and standards note

A disposal drains through a trap as the plumbing code requires, and its wiring falls under the electrical code, the National Electrical Code (NEC) as adopted in New Jersey. A repair that touches wiring should be left to a licensed professional.

Disposal calls come from Piscataway too, and a new unit goes in under plumbing installation while a sink that leaks at the faucet is a faucet repair job.

Keeping a disposal and its drain healthy

Run cold water before, during and for a short while after using the disposal so scraps clear the trap. Cut large scraps down, avoid pouring grease, and skip stringy or starchy items such as celery, potato peels and rice, which can pack into a paste. A handful of ice cubes now and then helps knock residue off the blades.

Cleaning a disposal with dish soap and warm water is better than harsh chemicals, which can damage seals and are a poor fit for older drains. If bad smells persist, the cause is often food trapped under the splash guard or in the drain behind it.

Dishwasher connections and the sink drain

The dishwasher drain hose usually connects to the disposal inlet. If the knockout plug was never removed after a new unit was fitted, the dishwasher will not drain. A hose that rises into a high loop prevents backflow, and one that sags can trap water and cause smells.

A leak that shows up only when the dishwasher runs is a hose or clamp issue, not the disposal. A plumber can trace it quickly and fix it while the sink cabinet is already open.
